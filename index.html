How I work

From a messy brief
to a clear next step.

Every project arrives with pressure from four directions: the business, the people using the product, the technology underneath, and the organisation making the calls. Above that sit the layers I work across, each with a few different tools. None of it runs in a fixed order, and no project needs all of it. What comes in at the bottom decides which tools get picked up on the way to the top.

Start from

  1. Whichever route the work takes, it ends in something a team can act on the next morning: what to build, why it matters, and how we'll know it worked.

    • Decision record
    • Tested prototype
    • Prioritised backlog
    • Success metrics

  2. Everything below is only useful if a team can repeat it. This layer covers the shared rituals, a record of what we've learned about users, and workflows where AI takes the repetitive parts.

    Team
    • Ways of working
    • Facilitation & workshops
    • Mentoring & teaching
    Tooling
    • Documentation & handoff
    • Shared insight library
    • AI-assisted workflows

  3. Shipping is where the question gets answered. With enough traffic I test properly. Without it, I look for the cheapest way to be less wrong.

    Experiments
    • A/B & multivariate testing
    • Metrics & reporting
    Quality
    • Usability testing
    • Accessibility review
    • Localisation QA

  4. The visible part. Because it rests on the decisions underneath, reviews tend to be about whether it works, which is a far more useful argument than taste.

    Structure
    • Information architecture
    • Design systems
    Surface
    • Interface & interaction design
    • Content & UX writing
    • Choice architecture

  5. Most of a project's value gets decided here, in what the team agrees to solve and what it agrees to leave for later.

    Problem
    • Problem framing
    • Behavioural diagnosis
    Bets
    • Hypotheses & success metrics
    • Trade-off mapping
    Scope
    • Scoping & appetite

  6. Before an opinion turns into a screen, I check what the evidence supports. Sometimes that takes a research round. Sometimes a session recording and two good questions are enough.

    People
    • User research
    • Behavioural analysis
    Data
    • Analytics & session data
    Context
    • Stakeholder interviews
    • Process & service mapping
    • Platform & constraint audit
    Science
    • Checking claims against the research

Speed moves the bottleneck

When a prototype takes an afternoon instead of a sprint, the slow part becomes knowing what to test. That's where the saved time goes.

Models repeat the folklore

Ask a model for UX best practice and it returns whatever gets repeated most, myths included. So I keep a graph of what the research actually backs.

Workflows the team can keep

An AI workflow that only I know how to run leaves when I do. I write each one down so the team can run it without me.
