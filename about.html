About

I've always been more comfortable with hard problems than easy briefs.

I'm Jose Romero, a senior product designer with 12 years of experience across digital products, conversion strategy, and design systems. I work in English and Spanish at professional level, and I'm currently based in Zaragoza, Spain, working as an embedded senior designer on IKEA's New Business Platform, part of the team building two peer-to-peer marketplaces from their early stages through European expansion.

Before that, I spent three years at Flat 101, one of Spain's leading digital consultancies, running UX and CRO work across four to eight concurrent enterprise accounts simultaneously. Santander Nordics, Meliá Hotels International, Mapfre, Mutua Madrileña, AXA Spain, IQOS, XTI. Different industries, different team structures, often at the same time.

None of that arc was planned. It started as 120-plus projects across print, branding, digital product, and CRO. Somewhere along the way the engagements got harder, longer, and fewer, until I ended up fully embedded inside one product team. Each stage just happened to set up the next one. The same systems thinking that made those complex engagements manageable is what I now use to design AI into a workflow properly, as its own layer, rather than treating it as a shortcut.

Garaje de Ideas / Groupe EDG: IKEA New Business Platform

2025–Present
Senior Product Designer (Implant)
Zaragoza, Spain (English environment)

Embedded full-time within IKEA's New Business Platform, an internal startup unit within Ingka Group, tasked with expanding IKEA beyond furniture into adjacent markets across Europe. The two products below are run by one team, and I work across both.

IKEA Home Services

A peer-to-peer marketplace connecting homeowners with verified professionals across 50+ home improvement categories. Launched in Madrid in September 2024, expanded to seven Spanish regions by mid-2026. Published figures: 500,000+ downloads, 21,000 service requests completed, 57,000 new or reactivated IKEA Family members, 4.9/5 customer satisfaction rating. This is the product I joined for: user flows, prototyping, in-store user research sessions at IKEA locations, and accessibility auditing, alongside product owners, service designers, engineers, content designers, and business developers. I still support it.

IKEA Second-hand Marketplace

Peer-to-peer resale of IKEA products, with IKEA's catalog as a data backbone for listings. Piloted in Madrid, now live in five European countries, with full European expansion committed. Most of my work goes here today: product design and cross-functional collaboration as the platform matures and scales.

Design operations

Built the team's ways-of-working system from scratch: a framework spanning Figma (source of truth), Jira, Confluence, and Phrase. Before this, the process lived mostly in people's heads. Now it's written down somewhere new hires can actually find it.

AI workflow design & facilitation

Facilitating Shape Up methodology sessions for the product team and designing the AI layer on top. The facilitation work led to a parallel body of work: designing AI-assisted workflows for the shaping process itself, including multi-agent orchestration approaches that distribute complex product features across specialized agents (problem decomposition, constraint mapping, solution evaluation). Outputs are structured to flow directly into the team's established Confluence documentation format. Also running AI literacy sessions for senior design professionals at Garaje de Ideas, focused on workflow design rather than tool use.

Factor Común

2016–2021
Co-founder & Design Lead
Havana, Cuba

At 23, while finishing a five-year degree in Visual Communication Design, I co-founded Factor Común, a registered design and communications studio in Havana. It started as freelance work with two colleagues and grew into a proper studio doing branding, editorial design, digital interfaces, audiovisual production, and campaign work for institutional clients across Cuba.

Running it meant doing a lot that had nothing to do with design itself: client relationships, project planning, chasing down suppliers, bringing in new business, all under real resource constraints. The scale of it is nothing like what I do now, but the underlying discipline hasn't changed. It's where I learned structured thinking and commercial reality, well before any formal training in design methodology.

ISDi / Universidad de La Habana

2018–2021
UI Design Professor
Havana, Cuba / Remote

Taught UI design at the Instituto Superior de Diseño, affiliated with the University of Havana, developing curriculum, facilitating studio sessions, and building shared understanding of complex concepts across students at different levels of ability and experience.

Teaching forces habits you don't expect to need later: spelling out things that feel obvious to you but aren't, reading a room and adjusting on the fly, getting a group to agree on something nobody came in agreeing on. Turns out those are exactly the skills enterprise facilitation runs on, and I've leaned on them ever since.

How I work today,
and what it grew from.

Most of my work now runs through AI: GitHub Copilot in VS Code at IKEA, and Claude for building, prototyping and writing. Underneath sit the tools I’ve used for years in design, research and experimentation. Pick a skill to see what it draws on, or switch to 2022 to see what changed.

2026: AI runs through most of the work, on top of the same foundations.

AI practice

  • Turning what AI produces into material a team can read, discuss and act on. Most of my facilitation now is structuring and decoding AI output so a team can take it in without overload and act on it.

  • Multi-agent workflows with hard human checkpoints, built on Shape Up. The full framework is in Designing with AI.

  • Writing the skills, project instructions and memory that make agents work the team’s way: tone, standards, and what to check before handing anything back.

  • Reports, decks and dashboards built by agents in VS Code, connected to Jira, Confluence and Microsoft 365. It has almost replaced my office tools.

  • Prototypes and production UI built with AI, with Figma connected to the code. This site is one of them.

  • Research tooling that grades its own evidence. BECO links 420 behavioural-science sources to 243 concepts in a Neo4j graph.

Foundations

  • End-to-end product design across web and mobile, from research and flows to prototypes and specs engineering can build from.

  • Hypotheses, A/B and multivariate tests, and results that hold up in front of stakeholders. I still run experiments in Optimizely and ContentSquare on the Second-hand Marketplace.

  • Defaults, friction, loss aversion and social proof applied to real design decisions, with BECO as the evidence behind them.

  • Moderated and unmoderated research, in-store sessions, heuristic reviews and WCAG audits, turned into direction a team can use.

  • Ways of working across Figma, Jira, Confluence and Phrase, and the stakeholder work that keeps product, engineering and business moving, in English and Spanish.
